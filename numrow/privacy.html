NumRow Keyboard

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

In short: NumRow does not collect, store, share or sell any data. What you type stays on your iPhone.

What NumRow collects

Nothing. NumRow has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code. Neither the app nor the keyboard sends any information to the developer or anyone else.

Full Access is optional

NumRow works fully without "Allow Full Access", and it is off unless you turn it on. With Full Access off, iOS blocks the keyboard from the internet, from sharing data with other apps and from reading your clipboard.

If you turn Full Access on (Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › NumRow), NumRow uses it for one thing only: haptic feedback when you tap a key, which iOS does not allow keyboards to do otherwise. iOS shows a general warning when you enable Full Access for any keyboard; for NumRow, nothing changes about your data. The keyboard contains no networking, storage or analytics code, so it cannot send or keep what you type. Because NumRow is open source, anyone can verify this.

How the keyboard uses your text

To work like a normal keyboard, NumRow briefly reads the text next to the cursor, on your device only, to:

  • capitalize the start of sentences and turn a double space into a period,
  • suggest corrections and completions using Apple's on-device spell checker,
  • apply your own Text Replacement shortcuts and avoid "correcting" your contacts' names.

This text is held in memory only while you type. It is never saved to storage, logged, learned from or sent anywhere.

Secure fields and banking apps

For passwords, PINs and other secure fields, iOS always switches to Apple's own keyboard, so NumRow never sees them. Many banking and e-wallet apps go further and allow only Apple's keyboard anywhere in the app; there too, iOS switches automatically and NumRow is never used.

Children

NumRow collects no data from anyone, including children.
